Where is the rest of Portugal's 2030 biomethane?

Action Plan 2030 figure against the pipeline, and what new manure plants could add at what cost

Answer. The Action Plan's 2030 figure is 2,720 GWh/yr. The pipeline of new and converted units that are operating or hold a title and public funding adds up to about 583 GWh/yr (21%); 26 of 54 registered units publish no size, and 10 have been rejected or duplicate another. That leaves about 2,137 GWh/yr. Manure cannot fill it at the Plan's prices: across the 18 areas with enough intensive livestock effluent, a plant on manure alone breaks even at €127–€936/MWh even scaled up with heat recovery; no area reaches the €88/MWh grant benchmark. The country's agro-industrial residues (330 kt/yr) bring 1 plant to €116/MWh or less, about 185 GWh/yr, and are then used up; of the 2 areas that receive them, 0 have that much reported by large food plants within 40 km (EEA register; smaller plants are not listed). Olive pomace helps in Mealhada, where the pomace a plant would need lies within 60 km (most of it now goes to extraction plants); elsewhere the areas with manure have little pomace nearby and the Alentejo areas with pomace have little manure. Land is the other limit: in Leiria, Alcobaça livestock already puts more than 170 kg N/ha on the farmland or the plant's digestate would need over half of it, so digestate must be exported or treated (not costed). Solid manure from the same farms would bring 8 to that price (1,134 GWh/yr) if that much exists, which no source shows. Manure plants under €116 can add a few hundred GWh at most: the rest of 2030 has to come from municipal biowaste, sewage sludge and crop-based feedstocks, or from support well above the Plan's benchmarks.

4. What else could fill the gap

The study puts the technical potential of all feedstocks at 2.74 TWh/yr. Outside livestock effluents, the streams it reports are:

agro-industrial residues330 ktPortugal 2023, APPB 2026
agricultural residues2,788 ktPortugal 2023, APPB 2026
organic municipal waste1,777 ktPortugal 2023, APPB 2026
sewage sludge587 ktPortugal 2023, APPB 2026
  • Municipal biowaste is the largest stream not yet tapped. Upgrading at existing waste digesters is cheap (Mota-Engil: €3.6–4.4 M per 3–5 MW unit); a new biowaste digester is not (Ambisousa: €17.7 M for about 13 GWh/yr). EU law has required separate biowaste collection since 2024, and from 2027 only separately collected biowaste counts as recycled (APPB 2026).
  • Sewage sludge is already digested at the large wastewater plants; the gas is used on site (see the conversion ranking). Thermal hydrolysis (Frielas, €28.7 M) raises yield.
  • Crop-based feedstocks (sequential crops, residues) need the RED sustainability rules checked; silage at €30/t is the farm-side co-substrate above.

None of these is modelled area by area here; they are the next screens.

5. Method and limits

  • Livestock by municipality from the 2019 census, scaled to the 2026 herd (INE semi-annual survey: dairy cows −13%, pigs +4%, applied nationally); effluent factors calibrated to ENEAPAI's 2019 intensive totals, so extensive grazing is excluded. Poultry litter, sheep and goats are not counted.
  • Areas are greedy circles of 15 km around municipality centroids; real catchments follow roads and farm clusters.
  • Plant cost: ADEME benchmark ratio (Class 5), the pilot's capex split rescaled on digester volume, gas flow and digestate mass (exponent 0.7); heat from the Braga climate normal for every area; connection 2 km where the municipality has a distribution network, else the mapped transmission line × 1.3.
  • Agro-industrial residues are a national total (APPB); no regional figure exists in the sources read (ENEAPAI covered livestock only).
  • Pipeline sizes: stated where a source gives them, else derived from MW at full load or call totals; 26 units unsized.
  • No promoter, farmer or operator was contacted.
