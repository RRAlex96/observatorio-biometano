Closing the 2030 accounts: the Action Plan line by line

Biomethane Action Plan (RCM 41/2024) Table 4 against owner reports, the pipeline of registered units, APA, INE and ENEAPAI data

Answer. The Action Plan's 2,720 GWh/yr for 2030 adds up from eight feedstock lines. On the evidence, the central case reaches about 1,443 GWh/yr (53%), with a low of 647 and a high of 3,653 GWh/yr; the high case is a set of explicit scenarios (every registered unit built at a typical size, the PERSU biowaste target met, cover crops on a tenth of the maize land), not a forecast. The shortfall of about 1,277 GWh/yr in the central case sits in agricultural residues (560 GWh); livestock effluents (473 GWh); methanation of biogenic CO₂ from upgrading with renewable hydrogen (290 GWh); organic fraction of municipal waste (new capacity) (265 GWh); existing biogas (landfills, waste and wastewater digesters) converted (183 GWh). Agro-industrial residues is at or above the Plan. Municipal biowaste and the unsized registrations are where the most volume could still appear. An independent check points the same way and further: on the four Floene distribution networks alone, producers have signed connection contracts for 1,290 GWh/yr (17 projects; about 836 GWh/yr outside the municipalities where existing plants are converting), with 1,377 GWh/yr more in discussion or analysis, while the 24 projects already registered at DGEG on Floene's networks (half of them hydrogen) add up to 906 GWh/yr; in the Norte, REN Portgás counts 505 GWh/yr from projects already in procedure (1,056 in its ambition case).

What it means for the numbers: the support model that decides most new plants' economics is still unpublished; ERSE's proposed connection share (60% by the gas system, capped at €600,000 and €20,000 per GWh/yr) would cut a typical project's connection bill but is not approved, so the model still charges it in full; gas forwards (2027 ≈ €69/MWh, 2028 ≈ €43/MWh) bracket the €45/MWh long-run merchant assumption; the retailer quotas (6% by 2030, announced, not law) would create the obligated demand the balance tests.

5. Method and limits

  • Existing biogas from the conversion ranking (owner reports for 29 of 34 plants); new manure plants from the 2030 gap screen; registered units from the pipeline (LNEG, DGEG 23-10-2026, PRR calls), sizes stated or derived as recorded there.
  • New registered units: low counts those operating or holding a DGEG title and public funding; central those with a DGEG title or a favourable environmental decision; high every live registration with a size. Sizes are stated by a source or derived from Nm³/h or MW, as recorded in the pipeline; environmental-impact studies supplied seven of them.
  • Biowaste yield from one plant (Ambisousa); no Portuguese plant publishes biomethane per tonne yet.
  • Cover-crop high case uses literature yields and an EU-study uptake share; it is a scenario.
  • Timing: a unit counted here is assumed to operate by 2030; several have 2027–2030 dates and none of the new units is verified as operating except Capwatt Aljustrel.
